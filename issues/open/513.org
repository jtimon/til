** OPEN #513 Consider postfix ! for Option values

Today ~items.get(i)!~ panics if the call throws, while ~maybe.unwrap()~
extracts the payload of ~Some~ and panics on ~None~. Would ~maybe!~ make
the common intent clearer than the explicit method name?

Consider a throwing ~lookup()~ that returns ~Option(T)~: today ~lookup()!~
still returns the Option, and ~lookup()!.unwrap()~ checks for absence too.
Could a reader tell which failure a bang handles without looking up the
signature? Any shorthand should preserve the payload's ownership and borrow
permissions just as ~unwrap()~ does.

Compare keeping ~unwrap()~, giving Option a built-in exception, and allowing
types to overload postfix ~!~. Would the shorter spelling justify a special
case? Would overloading make it harder to know what ~!~ does at a glance?
