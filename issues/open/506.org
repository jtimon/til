** OPEN #506 Explore proving til itself with til's own checker

Brainstorm state, split out of [[505.org][#505]]. Nothing here is approved or
actionable, and nothing can start before #505 produces a checker at all.

*** The wish

  The ideal end state of #505 is that til uses it on itself: properties of
  the compiler and of core are proved by til's own checker, not only
  tested. It is a separate issue because it is probably very hard, if it
  is even possible to maintain: every change to the compiler would have to
  keep its proofs alive. The #505 constraints carry over: our own thing,
  nothing vendored, an external tool at most as a temporary cross-check.

*** Precedents

  From memory, not re-checked; sizes are orders of magnitude.

  - CompCert: a C compiler written and proved in Rocq. Each pass is proved
    to preserve the meaning of the program. Several person-years, and the
    proofs are a few times larger than the compiler.
  - CakeML: an ML compiler proved in HOL4 that compiles itself, so the
    verified compiler binary comes out of the proof. The closest precedent
    for a self-hosting language.
  - seL4: an operating system kernel. Roughly twenty lines of proof per
    line of C, and keeping the proofs up to date is reported as the
    dominant cost of every later change. This is the maintenance worry in
    numbers.
  - Lean 4 and Rocq are NOT proved by themselves. Lean's compiler is
    written in Lean but unverified; what is trusted is a small kernel,
    re-checked by independent checkers. Separate projects verify a model
    of each kernel. The tools everyone trusts settled for a small kernel
    instead of a proved compiler.
  - Translation validation: do not prove the pass; check, on every run,
    that this particular output means the same as this particular input.
    CompCert does it for register allocation, Alive2 for LLVM
    optimizations. Far cheaper to maintain, because the pass can change
    freely as long as the checker still accepts its output.

*** A limit that does not go away

  A checker that proves its own kernel sound proves little by that alone:
  an unsound kernel can accept a false proof of its own soundness. Trust
  has to bottom out in something else -- a kernel small enough to read, or
  a second, independent checker. That is exactly the role the "temporary
  external cross-check" of #505 would play.

*** What could be proved, cheapest first

  1. Core containers and numbers (~src/core~, about 5k lines): no panic,
     ~safe_*~ really report every overflow, ~Vec~ / ~Map~ / ~Str~ behave
     as their mathematical models. Ordinary library verification; nothing
     compiler-specific.
  2. The checking kernel itself (direction 6 of #505), subject to the
     limit above.
  3. Structural invariants of single passes, not their full correctness:
     the desugarer leaves no sugar behind, the typer leaves no untyped
     node, the garbager destroys every owned value exactly once. The last
     is what ASAN samples today ([[464.org][#464]]); a proof would cover every
     program, not the test corpus.
  4. Meaning preservation of one pass. The constfolder is the natural
     first: it replaces a call by the value the compile-time evaluation
     gave, so the claim is "folding does not change the result".
  5. The whole compiler, down to the binary. ~src/self~ is about 76k
     lines, there are several backends that must agree, and the C backend
     ends in an unverified C compiler.

  Each rung needs the previous ones much less than it needs a written
  definition of what a til program MEANS. That definition does not exist
  (also an open question of #505) and from rung 4 on it is the first
  deliverable.

*** Specific to til

  - Bootstrap. The compiler that would check the proofs is built from
    ~boot/til.c~ by the previous compiler (doc/self.org). A proof about
    the source says nothing about a boot artifact produced by an
    unproved earlier compiler; CakeML's answer was to bootstrap inside
    the prover.
  - Several execution paths (interpreter, C, MLIR, FASM). A proof about
    one says nothing about the others unless they are shown to agree.
  - Rate of change. The compiler changes daily and much of it is written
    by bots. Proofs that break on unrelated edits would stop that, unless
    the proofs are also produced mechanically and only CHECKED by the
    kernel (the Bend 2 bet recorded in #505).

*** Ways to keep it maintainable (none chosen)

  - Stop at rungs 1-3: invariants and the library, never full
    correctness.
  - Translation validation instead of proved passes, where a checker for
    the pass output is simpler than the pass.
  - Proofs as generated artifacts: search, decision procedures or an LLM
    produce them, the kernel checks them, a failed proof is a failed
    build like a failed test.
  - Freeze and prove only what rarely changes (core, the kernel), and
    keep testing the rest.

*** Open questions

  - Is any rung worth its upkeep compared with what tests, ASAN and the
    reproducible two-pass build already give?
  - Which single property would be the first target, to measure the real
    cost before committing to more?
  - Where would proofs live, and would a broken proof fail ~make test~?
    (Any new file kind or build step needs approval.)
  - Does the trusted base end at the C compiler, or is a path without
    one a goal?

*** Possibly related issues

  Candidates only; none is confirmed as a dependency except #505.

  - [[505.org][#505]]: the checker itself. Hard prerequisite.
  - [[383.org][#383]] backend conformance: the backends agreeing is what makes one
    semantics meaningful.
  - [[272.org][#272]] garbager and [[461.org][#461]] last-use destruction: the pass whose
    invariant (destroy exactly once) is the most valuable to prove.
  - [[464.org][#464]] ASAN by default: the sampling evidence a proof of that
    invariant would replace or back up.
  - [[472.org][#472]] / [[473.org][#473]] deterministic and reproducible builds: evidence about
    the step from source to binary that no source-level proof covers.
  - [[453.org][#453]] FASM backend: a path to a binary without a C compiler in
    the trusted base.
  - [[474.org][#474]] native JIT: one more execution path that would have to agree
    with the others.
