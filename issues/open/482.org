** OPEN #482 Explore concurrency: Go channels, NNG, ZeroMQ, Jai and semaphores

Explore what the README's aspirational "Better concurrency than Go" could mean
for Til. "At some point, subjectively" is a goal, not a claim about current
capabilities.

*** Questions to explore

- Go channels and select: ownership, buffering, backpressure, cancellation,
  closure and how easy it is to reason about blocked tasks.
- NNG and ZeroMQ (ZMQ): message-passing patterns, in-process versus IPC/network
  boundaries, delivery semantics and integration with task scheduling. Start
  with Til's existing NNG integration rather than assuming a new dependency.
- Jai: investigate its concurrency approaches and examples, recording sources
  and distinguishing available facilities from proposed ideas.
- Semaphores, mutexes, condition variables and atomics: where explicit
  synchronization helps, and where higher-level primitives would help more.
- Task/thread lifetime, shutdown, error propagation, fairness and observability
  across these approaches.

*** Outcome

Compare the approaches against Til's current facilities and try a small Til
experiment with a concrete workload. State what "better" means (subjective
ergonomics, correctness, performance), and measure any performance claims.
Document ownership, lifetime and compatibility decisions, and which pieces
belong in the language, runtime, standard library or application, before
proposing an API.
