** FIXED #369 The generated C meets clang on linux, not only on the mac runners

Every linux job compiles the generated C with gcc: ~toolchain_command~
in src/self/targets.til hardcodes "gcc" for LinuxX64/LinuxX86 and names
"clang" only for MacosX64/MacosArm64/Wasm32. So the entire test corpus
is checked under ~-Wall -Wextra -Werror~ by exactly one compiler on
linux, and clang first sees it on the two slowest jobs in the run.

That is a real gap, not a theoretical one: clang under -Werror rejects
things gcc accepts in silence, and the emitter already carries four
separate workarounds for it, two of them charged to #25:

- src/self/builder.til:2354 -- -Wswitch-bool on the niche discriminant
  (a C comparison switched on directly), fixed with an explicit (int);
- :2432 -- a til switch with no ~case:~ needs an explicit C ~default:~,
  because clang rejects a constfolded scrutinee matching no case
  (~switch (5) { case 1: ... }~); first hit by test/flow.til;
- :3659 -- -Wgnu-null-pointer-arithmetic on the ~(U8 *)(NULL) + n~ form
  Apple clang refuses, now a standard (void *)(UPtr) cast;
- :5093 -- -Wtautological-compare on a two-constant tag compare (below).

Every one of them was found the same way: green linux, red mac. Two of
those comments say so in as many words.

The most recent one is the argument for filing this. e3085558 taught
the emitter to spell a bare variant name as its tag; where BOTH
operands of a tag compare were variant names -- ~match Color.Red
{ case Color.Red: ... }~, which test/match.til is full of -- the C
became ~Color_TAG_Red == Color_TAG_Red~. gcc said nothing, the suite
passed 356/356 on linux, and every master run from e3085558 to
59ee8153 was red on macos-arm64 and macos-x64 and on nothing else.
Locally the same failure took about two minutes to reproduce with
~clang -Wall -Wextra -Werror -fsigned-char -c gen/til/match.c~.

*** The cheap shape

The job that would host this already exists. ~macos (cross-compile on
linux)~ runs on ubuntu-latest, already apt-installs clang and lld,
already runs ~make~ for bin/til, and already drives clang through the
driver with ~--target=macos-arm64~ -- to compile ONE file,
examples/hello_script.til, in under two minutes. Pointing it at the
test corpus instead of a single example is the smallest version of
this, and it needs no compiler-side change at all.

Going through the driver is the point, not an implementation detail. A
hand-rolled ~for f in gen/til/*.c; do clang -Wall -Wextra -Werror ...~
sweep was tried while diagnosing 59ee8153 and 4 of 130 files failed for
reasons that have nothing to do with warnings: usize64.c,
usize64_comptime.c and usize64_sizes.c only compile at ~--usize=64~,
and bindgen_raylib.c is a bindgen artifact whose ~format~ has a
different signature. The driver already knows each file's flags; a
shell loop has to relearn them and will drift.

*** To settle before implementing

- Coverage: the whole test/ corpus cross-built for mac, or a subset?
  Cross-building compiles without running, so the cost is compile time
  only -- but the corpus is 130 files against the current job's one.
- Whether Wasm32 (also clang, also -Werror) is worth the same
  treatment, or whether the mac cross target covers the class already.
- Whether an explicit compiler override -- a ~CC~-style knob that
  ~toolchain_command~ consults -- is the better general answer, since
  it would also let a developer run the whole suite under clang locally
  instead of going through ~--target~. That is a new config surface and
  a separate decision from the CI job; the job needs no such knob.

*** What it would not catch

The mac natives stay regardless: this only covers what clang rejects at
COMPILE time. Darwin-runtime behaviour -- the ASAN and leak runs, the
Guard Malloc probe, and the terminal-state class #25 hit, where the
kernel raises PENDIN behind tcsetattr -- has no cross-compiled
equivalent, and those failures are exactly why the mac jobs exist.
